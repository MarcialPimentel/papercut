# Papercut — Game Design Document
_Status: design set as a mouse-driven 3D paper weapon fighting game (2026-11-01). Next: Milestone 1, "Feel test" (mouse vs the practice dummy). Source of truth for what the game is._

## Pitch
A 3D weapon fighting game in folded paper. Two paper samurai trade light and heavy slashes inside a pop-up notebook, fought with the mouse: every hit cuts the paper red, and anyone caught mid-attack is cut in half on the spot.

Art direction: [art-direction.md](art-direction.md) (the Notebook Dojo; playable mock-up in `art/papercut-mockup.html`). Style references, inspiration only (our own characters and world, nothing borrowed): Paper Mario (sticker outline), Samurai Jack (flat angular shapes), Kartana (folded white-and-red blade), South Park (cut paper), school stationery. Game references: weapon fighting games such as Samurai Shodown (few, heavy, decisive hits), Bushido Blade (one clean cut can decide it), and PC melee games such as Chivalry (mouse-driven swordplay, feints, ripostes). The counter cut, the cursor guard and the paper damage are ours.

## Player fantasy
A sharp duelist in a real fighting game: poke fast, read the rival, and punish the moment they get greedy, with one counter cut that splits them in half. Easy to learn, with a high skill ceiling.

## Core loop (30 seconds / one match / one session / long term)
- **30 seconds:** dance in and out of range, poke with light slashes, move the cursor to guard high or low, feint a heavy to bait them, and wait for the rival to commit or miss. Hit them mid-attack and it's a counter cut: instant K.O.
- **One match:** best of 5 rounds of 60 seconds. Every hit slices the rival's paper health strip and leaves a red cut on their body; a K.O. splits them. Between rounds the notebook's page turns to the next spread.
- **One session:** a few online matches against friends (since the first build), or an Endless run against bots that get harder every 5. Sumi doubles as a practice dummy; a full computer fighter comes later.
- **Long term:** new fighters with their own moves and specials, new blades and spreads, each a new Blender model and rig to test. Kept small on purpose: the game is also a test bed.

## Design pillars (every feature must serve one; in a conflict the lower number wins)
1. **Every cut shows:** every hit cuts the paper. Hits leave red slashes cut into the paper fighter, the health strip is paper that gets sliced shorter, and a K.O. cuts the fighter in two. Red appears only where paper is cut. No damage numbers.
2. **Feel first:** a slash must feel good (hitstop, shake, sound, slash line) before any new content is added.
3. **Read it at a glance:** one lane, side-on camera, clear wind-ups, the mouse plus a few keys. Guard height is readable from the blade alone, and every fighter keeps its outline against any paper.
4. **Pop-up notebook:** everything is folded or cut from stationery (graph, ruled, legal pad, marble covers, kraft card), in a real 3D book whose pages turn between rounds.
5. **Test bed for art:** every fighter and blade goes through one documented Blender → game pipeline, so models, rigs and animations are easy to try.

## Look (summary; full detail in art-direction.md)
- **Fighters:** papercraft samurai (since 2026-11-02): paper puppets of overlapping cut card, modelled in Blender to Marz's model guide. A thick ink line round every piece, a thin cream rim round the figure, flat colours with paper grain. The body faces the camera, the head is in profile toward the rival. Paper is red inside. Rigid stop-motion animation: pieces turn on pivots and snap between held key poses; turning around, the cut-out flips. Kami (player 1): indigo robe over a cream kimono. Sumi (player 2 / dummy): the same inverted, cream over indigo.
- **Player name (since the name build):** typed on the "Your fighter" screen (letters, numbers, spaces and _ . ' -, up to 14), with a tag under the preview. One name everywhere online: over the health strips in quick match and invite rooms, in tournaments (the lobby box changes the same name), Ranked and the Endless leaderboard. A first-time player, or anyone who empties the box, gets a "Ronin 4F2A"-style name, saved like a typed one. No word filter yet.
- **Player looks:** each player picks a colour set (worn dark or light), an outfit print and a sword style on the "Your fighter" screen. Looks never change the fight; one fighter always wears dark and the other light. Details in art-direction.md.
- **World:** an open notebook on a desk. Round 1 is fought on the Graph Paper Dojo spread, round 2 on the Ruled Courtyard, round 3 on the Legal Pad Grove; the match winner stands on the marble back cover.
- **HUD:** paper health strips that get sliced shorter, a paper countdown tag, round markers. Straight lines and flat facets only. Red is paper, not blood. One non-red accent per fighter.

## Sound (summary; full detail in [sound.md](sound.md))
- **Mix:** free CC0 recordings for the big moments (blade clash, cuts, K.O., page turn), code-made sounds for the rest. Every sound has a code-made fallback.
- **Sword and paper at once:** a clash rings like steel with a paper snap on top; hits are paper slices and tears, not blood or flesh. A clash, a block and a parry each sound different, so the ear reads the exchange too (pillar 3).
- **Round flow:** kabuki clappers for "Round N", a taiko hit for "Cut!", ticks in the last 10 seconds, stings for round and match results.
- **Music:** made in code (taiko, high drum, clappers, a temple bell, koto-like plucks and a bamboo flute on Japanese scales). Every page, the menu, Endless, tournaments and the back cover has its own track; Endless builds with each bot level. Within a fight it is busier when a round is close to deciding and silent from a K.O. to the next round.

## The fight (rules)
- **The lane:** both fighters move along one path across the spread and always face each other. The world, fighters and camera are fully 3D; the fight is on a line.
- **Guard stance:** the mouse sets it. **Flick** style (default, on trial): flick the mouse **up** to guard **high**, **down** to guard **low**; only the direction counts (about 12 px of quick movement one way), not where the mouse is, and no cursor is drawn. **Cursor** style (the earlier one, in Settings): a crosshair above your fighter's waist on screen is **high**, below it **low**. The blade follows. When not attacking, you guard at that height, even while walking.
- **Attacks** (both cut at your guard height; left click light, right click heavy):

  | Move | Speed | Damage | Notes |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Light slash | Fast wind-up, short recovery | 10% of the strip | Safe to throw; the main poke |
  | Heavy slash | Slower wind-up (0.25 s) with a step in at the end, long recovery | 35% of the strip | Blocked, it chips 3 and staggers the blocker's guard, so you keep the turn; while winding up you're open to a counter cut |

- **Block:** a slash that meets a guard at the same height is blocked (paper chips, both pushed apart). A blocked **heavy** instead staggers the blocker: their guard is knocked aside and the attacker recovers 4 frames (about 0.07 s) first, still in reach for a light. The blocker's next guess (or a parry) can still stop the follow-up.
- **Parry:** flick the cursor to the matching height in the last moment before impact; the attacker staggers and is open.
- **Riposte:** after a parry, your next slash (within about 0.6 s) is a riposte: light-slash speed, heavy damage, a lunge with extra reach, and it can't be parried.
- **Feint:** cancel a heavy during the first part of its wind-up, before the step-in (left click or Q), and snap back to guard. Fakes a heavy to bait the rival's counter cut, then punish their miss. Every heavy makes the same paper fold as its wind-up starts, real or feinted, and the cancel itself is silent, so the sound never tells them which it is.
- **Clash:** two slashes of the same kind and height at the same moment bounce off each other.
- **Counter cut (touch of death):** hit a fighter during a heavy's wind-up (15 frames), or in the first 6 frames (0.1 s) of recovery after any attack that missed, and it's an instant K.O. A hit later in a missed heavy's recovery is a normal hit. Two light slashes trading at once just do normal damage, so quick pokes never become a coin flip.
- **Hit stun:** a clean hit staggers the defender briefly (longer for heavies) and pushes them back. The hitter always recovers first: 2 frames after a light, 4 after a heavy.
- **Jump:** low slashes pass under a jumper once their feet are above the blade; slashes in the air cut high (and sail over a standing rival's head near the top of the jump, so swing on the way down), and nobody can block mid-air, so a high slash knocks a jumper down. Landing after an air slash leaves you stuck for 10 frames (no blocking), so a blocked jump-in can be punished.
- **Dash and backdash:** double-tap toward or away from the rival.
- **Rounds:** 60 seconds. A K.O. or the most strip left at time wins the round; a tie replays it. Best of 5 (first to 3 rounds). The page turns between rounds; rounds 4 and 5 reuse the graph and ruled spreads.
- **Custom rooms:** whoever creates an invite room picks the rules, and both players play by them: rounds (best of 1, 3, 5, 7 or endless), health (50, 80, 100 or 150) and the round timer (30, 60 or 90 seconds, or none). Quick match always uses the defaults (best of 5, 80, 60 s).
- **Tournament:** a room for 3 to 8 players with a knockout bracket (lose once and you're out; empty spots in a 4- or 8-player bracket are byes). The creator picks the rules (as for a custom room, but no endless rounds) and starts it when everyone is in. One fight at a time: everyone else watches it live, and everyone sees the bracket between fights. A watcher's Esc (or the controller's Menu button) shows the bracket over the live fight, with a "Back to the fight" button; Esc or B goes back. Only the bracket's "Leave tournament" button leaves. The server's own re-play of each fight decides who goes through (a game whose fight fingerprints disagree with the server's loses, as in ranked). A player who leaves loses their current or next fight. Players can change their name in the lobby (the same name as on the "Your fighter" screen). Between fights or after the final, the creator can deal a new bracket: the same players (anyone who left is dropped), seats shuffled again, at least 3 players.
- **Endless mode (solo):** one round per bot, one bot after another, all on one page: no page turns. When a bot is cut, the next one steps in a few paces away on your left or right (unpredictable), and you stay where you stood. Your strip carries over from bot to bot and refills by half every 5 bots; each bot starts with a full strip. Every 5 bots the bots level up: they read your wind-ups and block them (late enough to parry at high levels), counter-cut your misses, knock you out of the air, punish your landings and attack more often. One lost round ends the run. Score: bots cut; the best run is remembered in the browser and shown on the title screen.
- **Tutorial (solo, 2026-11-02):** a guided fight against a scripted Sumi, about 3 minutes, at full speed with the real rules. Nine steps, each waiting until you do the move, with a one-line paper prompt under the strips: guard high / low, light slash (cut under her guard), block (her glint shows when, her blade shows where), heavy and stagger (then cut at the other height), feint, jump a low slash, parry into riposte, counter cut on a heavy's wind-up (she winds up as her glint fades), and counter cut on a miss, where you swing at the air and Sumi cuts you for it. After 3 failed tries a hint shows; any step can be skipped (Tab or the button). Strips refill so nobody loses; a K.O. plays out and the fight starts fresh. Offered once on the first visit (a "New here?" card) and always on the title screen; "How to play" stays as the reference. Prompt words live in one file with the buttons as names, so pad prompts can swap in; the guard and parry words follow the guard style in Settings (Flick: "flick up / down"; Cursor: "crosshair above / below your waist"), and on a controller every prompt names pad buttons (left stick, RB, RT, B, A). Button names follow the player's own bindings. Skip is its own bindable action: Tab, or LT on a controller. On a controller the tutorial cards work like other menus (A picks, B leaves).
- **Camera:** side-on with a slight angle to show volume, framing both fighters. On a K.O. it swings to a three-quarter view with slow motion; on a page turn it pulls up to show the whole book.
- **Controls (the mouse fights, the keyboard moves; or a controller does both):**

  | Action | Mouse and keyboard | Controller (Xbox names; Steam Deck the same) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Guard high / low | Flick the mouse up / down (Flick style), or the crosshair above / below your fighter's waist (Cursor style) | Left stick (or d-pad) up / down. The guard stays where it was last pushed |
  | Light slash | Left click | RB or X |
  | Heavy slash | Right click | RT or Y |
  | Feint (during a heavy's wind-up) | Left click or Q | Light again, or B |
  | Move | A / D | Left stick (or d-pad) left / right |
  | Dash / backdash | Double-tap D / A | LB (the way the stick points; backdash when it's at rest), or double-tap the stick |
  | Jump | Space or W | A |
  | Guard low while held | (none; set one in Controls) | (none) |
  | Restart match | R | View |
  | Hitbox view on / off | H | (none) |
  | Tuning panel (Practice only) | T | (none) |
  | Menu | Esc | Menu |

  Every key, mouse button and controller button above can be changed on the **Controls** screen (main menu), saved with the other settings; Esc, the controller's Menu button, the left stick and the d-pad stay fixed. Menus work with the controller too: stick or d-pad to move, A to pick, B to go back. Prompts (How to play, "Press R") show the buttons of whichever device was used last. Touch screens get on-screen buttons (shown only when there's no mouse).

- **Opponents:** another person, online only (since the first build). No two players on one PC. Sumi works as a practice dummy that switches guard (a glint is the tell), swings back now and then and sometimes feints a heavy. A computer fighter comes in Milestone 4.
- **Roster:** Kami and Sumi share one move set in Milestone 1 (different looks), so the core feel is tuned once. Later each gets their own speed, reach and one or two specials done as direction + button (no motion inputs).

## Paper damage (approach)
- **Hits:** a clean hit leaves a red slash where the blade crossed, along the slash's angle, cut through every piece of paper it passes (torso, sleeves, arms or legs). Heavy hits leave longer, wider slashes.
- **Health strip:** a strip of paper in the HUD; each hit slices a piece off its end, which falls away. A full strip is 80 health: a light cut takes 10 (an eighth), a heavy or riposte 35, a blocked heavy chips 3.
- **K.O.:** every piece is cut in two along the blade's line; the white stops a hair short of the line, so a thin red edge shows. The top half flies off along the blade, the legs keel over. Earlier slashes stay on the halves.
- **Physics:** the halves are Rapier physics bodies (since 2026-11-02, #7): they tumble, bounce off pop-ups and lie flat on the page. Physics is cosmetic only: it never decides who wins, so online play only has to sync inputs. Halves and flecks clear when the page turns; in Endless the halves of the last 5 cut bots stay on the page and older ones fold flat into it.

## Platform and tech
- **Platform (since 2026-11-02):** Steam is the target. A desktop app for Windows and Linux (so it runs natively on Steam Deck), plus the same game on the website at papercut.marz.gg, played with the mouse and keyboard or a controller. Desktop and website players fight each other online.
- **Desktop app:** Electron (it ships its own Chrome, so WebGL and mouse lock behave the same on every PC and the Deck, and `steamworks.js` needs it for achievements and cloud saves). The game's files are inside the app, so Practice and Endless work offline. Windows ships as a zip folder (Steam does the install), Linux as a tar.gz. CI builds both on every PR. Click-by-click: [desktop.md](desktop.md).
- **Settings screen** (main menu, website and app): fullscreen or windowed, resolution scale, frame cap, vsync (app only), music and effects volume, guard style. Keys and controller buttons have their own **Controls** screen. The app saves them, with your name and best Endless run, in one `settings.json` file (ready for Steam Cloud); the website keeps them in the browser.
- **Versions online:** only players running the same fight rules are paired (a fingerprint of the rules' code, checked by the rooms server). An older app gets "update to play online" instead of a fight that falls out of sync; art, sound and menu changes don't lock anyone out.
- **Stack:** TypeScript, Vite, Three.js. Flat card pieces whose borders, slashes and K.O. cuts are worked out as 2D shapes (no custom shaders needed). Rapier (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`) for the cut halves and flecks, loaded in the background so the first fight isn't delayed.
- **Mouse lock:** during a fight the browser holds the mouse (Pointer Lock) so it can't drift out of the window; the game draws its own crosshair. Esc frees it (the browser's rule), and it is released when the match ends so Rematch and Leave can be clicked.
- **Mouse input:** the last flick up or down sets the guard (or, in Cursor style, the cursor against the fighter's waist each frame); only the resulting guard height and the clicks enter the rules, so the inputs stay tiny and identical for online play.
- **Controller input:** the browser's Gamepad API, read once a frame (`src/pad.ts`). Like the mouse, it only produces the guard height and the same buttons, so a controller player and a mouse player can fight online and nothing in the fight knows the difference. Whichever of the mouse and the stick moved last sets the guard. The mouse isn't held (Pointer Lock) while playing on a controller.
- **Fighting-game foundations:** the fight runs on a fixed 60-frames-per-second clock and is decided only by the players' inputs, in plain code with tests, separate from drawing. Every move is data (wind-up, active and recovery frames, reach, step-in, damage, hit stun). Hits are checked with simple hit areas measured from the drawn fighter: each fighter's body is a box (0.9 m wide, feet to head), and a slash lands when its blade tip passes the front of that box and its cut line (chest or knee height) crosses it. Press H in any fight to see them. Inputs are buffered for a few frames so a press slightly early still counts. Effects (camera, flecks, physics halves) are cosmetic and never affect the result.
- **Online (since the first build):** only inputs travel. A small rooms server pairs players (invite link, room code or quick match) and passes each frame's buttons to the other player; both browsers run the same fight and compare a fingerprint every second. Input delay of 2–8 frames, picked from each player's measured ping; the fight waits if the rival's input is late. Later: rollback to hide lag, and direct browser-to-browser links (WebRTC) to cut the server hop.
- **Art pipeline:** Blender → glTF (`.glb`): flat pieces, one colour per shape, one pin per piece (`art/fighters/puppet.blend` → `public/models/puppet.glb`). The game adds thickness, the border, the red inside and the cut marks. Click-by-click: [blender-pipeline.md](blender-pipeline.md).
- **Tuning panel (since 2026-11-02, #9):** in Practice, T opens a legal-pad sheet of sliders for the fight's numbers (each move's frames, damage, chip, hit stun, hitstop, step-in and push; the parry, riposte, feint, counter-cut-after-a-miss, block-stagger, buffer and landing windows; the other hitstops; the guard line's mouse travel and dead zone; screen shake and K.O. slow motion). The fight pauses while it's open; changes apply at once and are kept in that browser for Practice. "Copy numbers" copies the changes, and Claude makes them the game's own numbers in a PR. Reach and body size have no sliders: they match the drawn samurai. Online fights, watching and Endless always reset to the game's numbers, so both players run the same fight.
- **Server referee (since the stats build):** the rooms server steps its own copy of every online fight from the same inputs it passes along, so it knows each result and what happened without trusting either browser. This is what the stats use now, and what ranked results will use.
- **Match stats:** every finished online match (quick match, invite rooms, tournaments) is recorded: rules, build, each player's guard style (Flick, Cursor or a controller), who won, how each round ended, and what every slash did (thrown, landed, blocked, parried, missed, clashed, feinted), plus parries, blocks, jumps, dashes, counter cuts and whether a blocked heavy's stagger was followed by a cut. Each match's inputs are kept for 90 days so new questions can be answered by re-playing old fights. Endless runs are reported by the browser (bots reached, what ended the run) until the leaderboard build re-plays them. No personal data. Anyone can read them at https://papercut.marz.gg/stats, filtered by build, rules (standard / custom / all) and guard style.
- **Ranked (since the ranked build):** a "Ranked" button next to "Find a fight" (which stays unranked). Standard rules only. A player is an anonymous token the browser keeps (the Steam ID once Steamworks is in); the server stores only a hash of it, with a name, a Glicko-2 rating and the number of matches. One queue for everyone: it pairs close ratings at once (within 100 points) and accepts 25 more points for every second of waiting, so anyone is paired within 30 seconds. Results come only from the server's re-play: a browser cannot report a result, and a browser whose fight fingerprints disagree with the server's (a modified game) loses the match. Leaving before the end is a loss, except when the server itself restarts (a deploy): then the match is called off and no one's rating changes. Only the first input a game sends for each frame counts, and only that one reaches the rival, so both always fight the server's fight. If the database can't be read, ranked waits rather than starting anyone from a fresh rating. No rematch: queue again. The first 5 matches are placement, then the paper-grade divisions by rating: Tissue (below 1350), Newsprint (1350), Cardstock (1475), Washi (1600), Gold Leaf (1750). Players see their division and points, with a division badge next to names in a fight.
- **Endless leaderboard (since the leaderboard build):** each player's best run (most bots, then fastest), top 100, for the last 24 hours, the last 7 days and all time, with your own place. The Endless bot's dice come from a numbered sequence (the run's seed), so a run is its seed plus the player's inputs. The browser sends those, the server re-plays the run in small slices (so online fights never wait) and posts only the score the re-play reaches, so an edited score is refused. Every re-played run also feeds the balance stats. Shown in a Leaderboard screen, in the title screen's leaderboard panel (with a Ranked tab: top placed players by rating), on the title screen next to your best run ("#14 on the leaderboard") and at the end of a run ("Leaderboard: #3 today, #14 all time"). Players are the same anonymous browser identity as Ranked, with the name from tournaments. Later: mirrored to a Steam leaderboard (Steamworks issue).
- **Deploy:** one Docker container on the hub server shared with Hormigas, at https://papercut.marz.gg (serves the game, the rooms and /stats). The stats live in Papercut's own database on the hub's shared Postgres, which the hub backs up every night (kept 7 days). Every merge to `main` deploys automatically, with a health check and automatic rollback. Details: [deploy.md](deploy.md).

## Scope for the first playable (Milestone 1: "Feel test")
The one question it answers: **does mouse-driven paper swordplay feel good?** Since the first build it can also be played person against person online (input delay; rollback comes in Milestone 2).
- **In:** the 3D notebook with three round spreads and the back cover; Kami and Sumi built from code shapes with outlines, red insides and cut marks; mouse controls (cursor guard, clicks) with keyboard movement; Sumi as a practice dummy that switches guard, swings and feints; guard stance, light and heavy slashes, block, parry, riposte, feint, clash, counter cut, hit stun, jump, dash and backdash; paper health strips, 60-second timer, best of 5 rounds with page turns; K.O. split with Rapier halves; hitstop, shake, slash line, paper sounds, K.O. camera with slow motion; R to restart; an on-screen tuning panel for the feel numbers (frame timings, damage, parry window, counter window).
- **Also in (first build):** online fights by invite link, room code or quick match; rematch. Since then: custom room rules, Endless mode and tournaments (2026-11-01).
- **Out:** rollback, two players on one PC, gamepads, menus beyond the start screen, the computer fighter, specials, different move sets, Blender art, more fighters or blades.
- **Done when:** Marz can play ten minutes against the dummy with the mouse and the guard, slashes, feints, parries and counter cuts all feel good, and the tuned numbers are saved as defaults.

## Roadmap (issue-level plan in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md))
1. **Milestone 1, Feel test (mouse vs dummy).** The first playable above.
2. **Milestone 2, Online fights.** Online play already works with input delay (first build); this milestone adds rollback, direct browser-to-browser links and online polish (reconnects, ping display).
3. **Milestone 3, Blender fighters.** Started 2026-11-02: Kami and Sumi are Blender cut-out puppets with a click-by-click guide. Still to come: key poses made in Blender, then new fighters and blades.
4. **Milestone 4, Solo and character.** A computer fighter, Kami and Sumi made different (speed, reach), one or two specials each.
5. **Milestone 5, The full notebook.** Menus, more spreads and blades.

## Risks and how we'll test them
- **Game feel is the whole game (Milestone 1):** a weak slash sinks a fighting game. Tuning panel and dummy playtests from the first build.
- **PvP is untested until Milestone 2:** with no local two-player, person-vs-person fun can only be judged once online works. Keep Milestone 2 right after Milestone 1, and keep the dummy smart enough (feints, swings back) to stand in until then.
- **Mouse guard precision:** guarding by cursor height could feel twitchy. The waist line, a small dead zone and a visible guard cue are tuned in Milestone 1.
- **Counter cut balance:** a touch of death can feel cheap. Tune the windows in Milestone 1 so it only ever punishes a visible mistake (a slow wind-up or a miss).
- **Online play (Milestone 2):** the biggest technical job. Reduced up front by input-driven, fixed-frame rules in Milestone 1.
- **Fighting-game scope:** move sets, hit areas, a computer fighter and a roster grow fast. One shared move set in Milestone 1; differences, specials and the CPU wait for Milestone 4.
- **Readability in 3D:** one lane, side-on camera, outlines on every part; check wind-ups at the widest zoom and in the night spreads.
- **3D art cost (Milestone 3):** keep parts low-poly and let the game add outlines, the red inside and cut marks.

## Open questions (for later milestones)
- Exact frame timings, damage, chip, parry window and counter window. (Tuned in Milestone 1; record the final numbers here.)
- Does a backdash have invincible frames?
- Each fighter's specials and differences (Milestone 4).
